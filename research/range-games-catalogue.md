# Practice Games and Drills by SG Category

**Question:** What established practice Games and Drills exist for each SG Category, and which can be self-scored by eye with only the Facility they need?

**Researched:** 2026-11-09. Terms follow `GLOSSARY.md`.

## Summary

| SG Category | Games | Numeric score and by eye as published (Yes or Markers) | Drills | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Off the Tee | 6 | 4 | 3 | Adequate. The strongest games are all variations on one idea: count shots inside a marked corridor. |
| Approach | 6 | 1 | 4 | **Thin.** See below. |
| Around the Green | 9 | 8 | 4 | Strong. Par 18 is independently described by several sources. |
| Putting | 12 | 10 | 5 | Strong. |

- **Approach is where the evidence runs short.** I found only one Game with a published numeric score that a competent golfer could score by eye (Eighteen), and I saw that one only through a search excerpt. The validated tests need a launch monitor or have no published scoring I could read. Original Approach templates will have to be built on general concepts rather than adapted from an established game.
- **Short-game and putting Games suit eye-scoring best,** because tees can mark rings and gates and because holing out needs no measuring.
- **Bunker play has no Facility in the glossary.** Bunker Games and Drills need a practice bunker, which is not a range bay, chipping area or putting green. Abby Parsons on PGA.com includes a bunker among the stations around the chipping area (G-AG5), so one option is to count it as part of the chipping area. This is a decision for the glossary.
- **Time is not published.** No source states a duration for any Game, so every Game is marked unknown.

## How to read this catalogue

**Evidence tags**

- **[Read]**: I read the page or paper text.
- **[Excerpt]**: Only a search-engine excerpt of the page was available, because the site refused direct fetching (Golf Digest, PGA.com, GolfWRX, Titleist). Treat these rules as less certain and check them before relying on details.

**Classification.** Items are classified by the glossary, not by the source's wording. Sources often call scored activities "drills". Anything with a numeric score is listed as a Game, and anything without one as a Drill unless noted.

**Eye-scoring verdict.** This is my judgement, not a sourced claim. "Yes" means the outcome is a stroke count, a holed putt or a ball clearly inside or outside a visible boundary. "Markers" means it needs tees, rings or range signs to judge. "Partly" means distance bands must be estimated. "No" means the published form needs measuring equipment.

**Scaling parameters.** "(source)" means the source itself proposes the change. "(inferred)" means it is my suggestion from the Game's structure.

**Unknown** means the source does not state the field.

## Off the Tee

Range bay Games depend on existing flags and signs, since golfers generally cannot place their own markers downrange (inferred). GolfLink and Golf Digest both use range signs or boundaries to define the fairway.

### Games

**G-OT1 Fairway Count** (published as "Fairways In Regulation")
- Rules: Pick two range boundaries with a fairway between them; hit 10 tee shots.
- Scoring: Percentage or count of shots in the fairway.
- Balls: 10. Time: unknown. Facility: range bay.
- Scale: fairway width, shot count (inferred).
- By eye: Yes.
- Source: [Golf Digest, The 15 best golf practice games](https://www.golfdigest.com/story/the-15-best-golf-practice-games) [Excerpt]

**G-OT2 Club-Weighted Fairway** (published as "Tee Shot Test")
- Rules: Create an imaginary fairway about 30 yards wide; hit 10 shots.
- Scoring: Per shot finishing in the fairway, 1 point with an iron, 2 with a hybrid or fairway wood, 3 with a driver.
- Balls: 10. Time: unknown. Facility: range bay.
- Scale: fairway width, club mix, shot count (inferred).
- By eye: Yes.
- Source: [Practical Golf, 5 Golf Games](https://practical-golf.com/golf-drills) [Read]

**G-OT3 Five-Ball Fairway** (published as "Create a Fairway")
- Rules: Mark a fairway mentally between yardage signs, straight or slightly doglegged; hit five balls.
- Scoring: Count in the fairway; target three, four or five.
- Balls: 5. Time: unknown. Facility: range bay.
- Scale: target count (source); fairway width (inferred).
- By eye: Yes.
- Source: [GolfLink, 9 Driving Range Games](https://www.golflink.com/instruction/best-range-games) [Read]

**G-OT4 Alternating-Club Driver Points** (published as "100")
- Rules: Hit 10 drives, playing another club between each, at a fairway 30 to 35 yards wide. The alternation is meant to feel more like the course.
- Scoring: +10 for a fairway, -5 for just outside it, -10 for a bigger miss. Perfect score 100.
- Balls: 10 drives plus the interleaved shots (count unknown). Time: unknown. Facility: range bay.
- Scale: fairway width, penalty size (inferred).
- By eye: Yes, though "just outside" needs a defined rough width (inferred).
- Source: [GolfWRX, Game of the Weekend: 100](https://golfwrx.com/327676/game-of-the-weekend-100/) [Excerpt]

**G-OT5 Corridor Distance Bands** (published as "Wood Shot")
- Rules: Five shots toward a flag, staying within boundary lines 50 metres wide.
- Scoring: 1 point for striking the ball; 2 for resting 60 to 90 m inside the corridor; 3 for 90 to 120 m; 4 beyond 120 m.
- Balls: 5. Time: unknown. Facility: range bay (needs marked boundaries and cones).
- Scale: corridor width, band distances (inferred). Bands are set for a beginner field and would need raising (inferred).
- By eye: Partly.
- Source: [Special Olympics PA, Level I Individual Skills Competition Rules](https://specialolympicspa.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/Level-1-Individual-Skills-Compeition-Rules.pdf) [Read]

**G-OT6 Drive Grid Points** (Drive, Chip and Putt; named program, see below)
- Rules: Three drives into a grid 40 yards wide, with flags or lines marking the boundaries.
- Scoring: 0 outside the grid; otherwise 1 to 25 points by distance, in 10-yard bands from 100 yards.
- Balls: 3. Time: unknown. Facility: range bay.
- Scale: grid width, attempts (inferred).
- By eye: Partly. 10-yard bands are hard to judge without measurement (inferred).
- Source: [Drive, Chip and Putt, Rules and Regulations](https://www.drivechipandputt.com/rules-and-regulations) [Read]

### Drills

**D-OT1 Three-Tee Line**
- Purpose: Reduce hitting up too much on the driver; flatter path and more centred strikes.
- Setup: Tee a ball as normal; push two more tees directly in front of it on the target line and swing through them.
- Balls: unknown. Facility: range bay (the source does not name one; inferred).
- Source: [GOLF.com, Bernie Najar (Top 100 Teacher)](https://golf.com/instruction/driving/simple-drill-solid-center-face-strikes/) [Read]

**D-OT2 Tee Gate Flight**
- Purpose: Repeatable tee-shot placement.
- Setup: Two tees about 20 yards out and roughly ten feet apart; fly the drive between them.
- Balls: unknown. Facility: range bay, if the bay permits placing markers 20 yards out (inferred; many will not).
- Source: [PGA.com, Brendon Elliott, Nine Ways to Play Better Golf Tee-to-Green](https://www.pga.com/story/nine-ways-to-play-better-golf-tee-to-green) [Excerpt]

**D-OT3 Face Strike Check** (published as "Impact Location Challenge")
- Purpose: Learn strike tendency, then deliberately find the centre of the face.
- Setup: Spray the driver face with an aerosol so impact shows, or mark the ball with dry-erase pen for irons. Note strike pattern, then deliberately strike different parts of the face.
- Balls: unknown. Facility: range bay.
- Source: [Practical Golf, 5 Golf Games](https://practical-golf.com/golf-drills) [Read]

## Approach

**The evidence here is thin.** Most published "range games" either need a partner, have no numeric score, or need a launch monitor. G-AP1 is the only one that fits as published. G-AP2 is partly eye-scored and set for beginners, and G-AP3 and G-AP4 need a score added.

### Games

**G-AP1 Eighteen**
- Rules: 18 shots in a set club order, each at a target, changing club every shot. The club list runs from 4-iron or hybrid through pitching and sand wedge.
- Scoring: Count of shots that would have finished on an average-size green.
- Balls: 18. Time: unknown. Facility: range bay.
- Scale: target size per club, acceptable outcome, club list (source).
- By eye: Yes, using a flag or sign as a green-sized target (inferred).
- Source: [GolfWRX, Game of the Weekend: Eighteen](https://golfwrx.com/330732/game-of-the-weekend-eighteen/) [Excerpt]

**G-AP2 Iron Corridor Bands** (published as "Iron Shot")
- Rules: Five shots toward a flag, within boundary lines 35 metres wide.
- Scoring: 1 for striking the ball; 2 for resting between 30 and 60 m; 3 for 60 to 90 m; 4 beyond 90 m, all inside the corridor.
- Balls: 5. Time: unknown. Facility: range bay (needs marked boundaries and cones).
- Scale: corridor width, band distances (inferred). Tests distance in a corridor more than proximity to a target, so it is a weak fit for Approach without change (inferred).
- By eye: Partly.
- Source: [Special Olympics PA, Level I Rules](https://specialolympicspa.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/Level-1-Individual-Skills-Compeition-Rules.pdf) [Read]

**G-AP3 Target Knockout** (published as "Knockout")
- Rules: Pick all the range targets. Hit the closest first, then the next farthest. Miss any and restart from the closest.
- Scoring: None published. A numeric score would have to be added, such as targets cleared or attempts taken (inferred).
- Balls: unknown, variable. Time: unknown. Facility: range bay.
- Scale: what counts as a hit, number of targets (inferred).
- By eye: Yes, once a hit is defined (inferred).
- Source: [GolfLink, 9 Driving Range Games](https://www.golflink.com/instruction/best-range-games) [Read]

**G-AP4 Club Climb** (published as "Every Club in Play")
- Rules: On a straight imaginary fairway, start with the highest-lofted club and hit it within bounds. If successful, go to the next club, working up to driver. Out of bounds restarts from the top.
- Scoring: None published; highest club reached or attempts taken could be used (inferred). Spans Approach and Off the Tee.
- Balls: unknown, variable. Time: unknown. Facility: range bay.
- Scale: fairway width, club list (inferred).
- By eye: Yes.
- Source: [GolfLink, 9 Driving Range Games](https://www.golflink.com/instruction/best-range-games) [Read]

**G-AP5 Approach-Iron Skill Test** (named research instrument, see below)
- Rules: 27 shots, three at each of nine targets, in randomised order, on a specially built range.
- Scoring: Each shot earns up to 3 points by percentage error index; maximum 81. Elite players scored 56.3 and 58.5 across two rounds; high-level amateurs 46.0 and 46.1.
- Balls: 27. Time: unknown. Facility: specially constructed range bay.
- Scale: target distance, tolerance per point (inferred).
- By eye: No as published; a launch monitor measures carry. Replacing the error index with a zone count around a flag would be my adaptation, not a validated form (inferred).
- Source: [Robertson, Burnett & Newton 2013, European Journal of Sport Science](https://vuir.vu.edu.au/26277) [Read]

**G-AP6 Nine-Ball Skills Test** (named research instrument, see below)
- Rules: Nine shots combining trajectory (straight, fade, draw) and height (normal, high, low) at an individually determined target area.
- Scoring: Unknown. I could not reach the full paper.
- Balls: 9. Time: unknown. Facility: range bay.
- Scale: target area size (inferred).
- By eye: Unknown; shape and height could be judged by eye only roughly (inferred). The informal version is "Tiger's Nine Shots", a checklist with no score ([GolfLink](https://www.golflink.com/instruction/best-range-games) [Read]).
- Source: [Robertson et al. 2012, Journal of Sports Sciences](https://vuir.vu.edu.au/26278/) [Excerpt]; [Robertson, Burnett & Gupta 2014](https://vuir.vu.edu.au/26267/) [Read] found either this or the Approach-Iron test predicted tournament approach-iron performance in 24 high-level golfers.

**Described without a numeric score.** Playing simulated holes on the range is recommended widely, but none of these publishes a score: [Abby Parsons, PGA.com](https://www.pga.com/story/three-great-practice-games-to-help-you-play-better-golf-under-pressure) [Excerpt], [GolfLink, Create a Hole](https://www.golflink.com/instruction/best-range-games) [Read], and [Alpenfels and Christina via GOLF.com](https://golf.com/instruction/10-proven-ways-make-range-practice-stick/) [Read].

### Drills

**D-AP1 Short, Middle, Long**
- Purpose: Control carry across adjacent clubs.
- Setup: Pick a target and hit your normal club; then the next longer club aimed 10 yards farther on the same line, and the next shorter club aimed 10 yards shorter.
- Balls: 3. Facility: range bay.
- Source: [GolfLink, 9 Driving Range Games](https://www.golflink.com/instruction/best-range-games) [Read]

**D-AP2 Random Club Selection**
- Purpose: Avoid getting too comfortable on the range; practise as you play.
- Setup: Select a club unpredictably, choose a suitable target, hit, repeat.
- Balls: unknown. Facility: range bay.
- Source: [GOLF.com, Alpenfels and Christina, tip 2](https://golf.com/instruction/10-proven-ways-make-range-practice-stick/) [Read]

**D-AP3 One Club, Many Targets / One Target, Many Clubs**
- Purpose: Vary the task to mirror the course.
- Setup: Hit one club at targets at different distances, or one target with a range of clubs including driver.
- Balls: unknown. Facility: range bay.
- Source: [GOLF.com, Alpenfels and Christina, tips 4 and 5](https://golf.com/instruction/10-proven-ways-make-range-practice-stick/) [Read]

**D-AP4 Wedge Calibration**
- Purpose: Tighten carry by swing length; learn each wedge's numbers.
- Setup: For each wedge hit half, three-quarter and full swings, 5 to 10 shots each, and track carry. The writer's session took about 45 minutes. A Titleist instructor describes 3 to 5 balls per clock position, averaging the carry ([Titleist, Mark Blackburn](https://www.titleist.com/videos/instruction/dial-in-your-distance-wedges) [Excerpt]).
- Balls: roughly 15 to 30 per wedge. Facility: range bay. The GOLF.com writer used a launch monitor; without one, carry can only be estimated.
- Source: [GOLF.com, Maddi MacClurg, Dustin Johnson's wedge combine](https://golf.com/instruction/dustin-johnson-wedge-combine-drill-learned/) [Read]

## Around the Green

Most Games here assume a chipping area next to a green with a hole, so the ball can be putted out.

### Games

**G-AG1 Par 18**
- Rules: Choose nine spots around the green with an even mix of easy, medium and hard shots. One ball, one attempt each, no practice swings or restarts. Get up and down.
- Scoring: Par 2 per spot, 18 total; lower is better. Record the score; play once a day.
- Balls: 1 moved around, or 9 ([18Birdies](https://help.18birdies.com/article/489-5-chipping-games-to-improve-your-short-game-video)). Time: unknown. Facility: chipping area with a green.
- Scale: spot difficulty mix, lies (source); distances (inferred).
- By eye: Yes.
- Source: [Golf Digest Middle East, Jack Woods (PGA Teaching Professional)](https://golfdigestme.com/?p=28221) [Read]; [Practical Golf](https://practical-golf.com/the-ultimate-practice-game-for-your-short-game) [Read]; [18Birdies](https://help.18birdies.com/article/489-5-chipping-games-to-improve-your-short-game-video) [Read]

**G-AG2 Scatter Up-and-Down** (published as Dave Phillips' "21 Game"; named, see below)
- Rules: Scatter nine balls around the green and play each from where it lies, including bunker shots, through to holing out.
- Scoring: Perfect 18; target 21, meaning six of nine up and down.
- Balls: 9. Time: unknown. Facility: chipping area with a green, optional bunker.
- Scale: downhill and sidehill lies, rough, longer putts, someone else scattering the balls (source).
- By eye: Yes.
- Source: [Titleist, Dave Phillips](https://www.titleist.com/videos/instruction/21-game-for-better-short-game-practice) [Excerpt]

**G-AG3 Rings** (published as "Darts")
- Rules: Tee rings around a target on the green; play a set number of shots.
- Scoring: Practical Golf has rings at 3, 6, 9 and 12 feet, scoring 1 to 4 from outer to inner, and 5 for holing out. 18Birdies gives 1 to 3 points by proximity (within 3 to 5, 2 and 1 feet), 10 for holed, with optional 5 within 6 inches and -2 for missing the green.
- Balls: 9 to 18 (18Birdies). Time: unknown. Facility: chipping area with a green.
- Scale: ring radii, ball count, lies (source for "zones and points can be adjusted").
- By eye: Markers (tees).
- Source: [Practical Golf, Chipping Drills](https://practical-golf.com/chipping-drills) [Read]; [18Birdies](https://help.18birdies.com/article/489-5-chipping-games-to-improve-your-short-game-video) [Read]

**G-AG4 15 Chips**
- Rules: Five balls each from easy, medium and hard chip shots.
- Scoring: 1 point per ball stopping within two club-lengths of the hole.
- Balls: 15. Time: unknown. Facility: chipping area with a green.
- Scale: scoring radius, shot difficulty (inferred).
- By eye: Markers (a club laid down).
- Source: [Golf Digest, The 15 best golf practice games](https://www.golfdigest.com/story/the-15-best-golf-practice-games) [Excerpt]

**G-AG5 Up-and-Down Stations**
- Rules: Set tee stations around the chipping area with different lies, grasses or a bunker. Three balls per station. An up-and-down counts only if you reach the green or chip in and then hole the putt. You cannot leave a station until you score two or better in two of three attempts (the exact meaning of "two or better" is unclear from the excerpt).
- Scoring: Count of up-and-downs; can be read as attempts needed (inferred).
- Balls: 3 per station, with repeats. Time: unknown. Facility: chipping area, optional bunker.
- Scale: station difficulty, pass threshold (inferred).
- By eye: Yes.
- Source: [PGA.com, Abby Parsons](https://www.pga.com/story/three-great-practice-games-to-help-you-play-better-golf-under-pressure) [Excerpt]

**G-AG6 Five-Ball Streak** (published as "5 Ball")
- Rules: Place five balls in various spots; get all five up and down in a row before leaving.
- Scoring: None published; attempts taken or best streak could be used (inferred).
- Balls: 5 per attempt. Time: unknown. Facility: chipping area with a green.
- Scale: ball count, spot difficulty (inferred).
- By eye: Yes.
- Source: [18Birdies](https://help.18birdies.com/article/489-5-chipping-games-to-improve-your-short-game-video) [Read]

**G-AG7 Chip Proximity Table** (Drive, Chip and Putt; named program, see below)
- Rules: Three chips to a scoring hole, set at about 10 to 15 yards unless varied. Putter and tee prohibited.
- Scoring: Per chip: 1 for more than 10 feet, 2 for 8 to 10, 5 for 6 to 8, 10 for 4 to 6, 15 for 2 to 4, 20 for 2 feet or nearer, 25 for holed.
- Balls: 3. Time: unknown. Facility: chipping area with a green.
- Scale: chip distance (source allows it); attempts (inferred).
- By eye: Markers. Seven bands means many tees (inferred).
- Source: [Drive, Chip and Putt, Rules and Regulations](https://www.drivechipandputt.com/rules-and-regulations) [Read]

**G-AG8 Chip and Pitch Circles** (published as "Chip Shot" and "Pitch Shot")
- Rules: Chip: five shots from 14 m to a hole, with circles of 3 and 6 m radius. Pitch: five shots over a 1 m high barrier 5 m away to a target 10 m away.
- Scoring: Chip: 1 for striking the ball, +1 inside 6 m, +1 inside 3 m, +1 holed; maximum 20. Pitch scores by landing and coming to rest in the target circle.
- Balls: 5 each. Time: unknown. Facility: chipping area for the chip; the pitch needs a barrier and the source suggests a range.
- Scale: circle radius, distance (inferred). The strike point adds nothing for a competent golfer (inferred).
- By eye: Markers. The pitch text refers to both 6 m and 12 m circles, so the rule needs clarifying.
- Source: [Special Olympics PA, Level I Rules](https://specialolympicspa.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/Level-1-Individual-Skills-Compeition-Rules.pdf) [Read]

**G-AG9 Bunker Circle** (published as "Bunker Shot")
- Rules: Five shots from a bunker over a face at least 1 m high to a target about 10 m away.
- Scoring: 1 for striking the ball, +1 for clearing the face between poles, +1 for landing in the circle and rolling out or landing outside and finishing inside, +1 for landing and finishing inside; maximum 20.
- Balls: 5. Time: unknown. Facility: practice bunker (not a glossary Facility).
- Scale: circle size, distance (inferred).
- By eye: Markers. As with the pitch, the source mixes a 6 m radius with a "12-meter circle".
- Source: [Special Olympics PA, Level I Rules](https://specialolympicspa.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/Level-1-Individual-Skills-Compeition-Rules.pdf) [Read]

### Drills

**D-AG1 Headcover Behind the Ball** (credited to Butch Harmon)
- Purpose: Fix chunked chips with clean contact.
- Setup: Empty headcover about one foot behind the ball; avoid it through the chip.
- Balls: unknown. Facility: chipping area.
- Source: [Practical Golf, Chipping Drills](https://practical-golf.com/chipping-drills) [Read]

**D-AG2 Alignment-Stick "Y"** (credited to Jon Hearn)
- Purpose: Stop the wrists flipping through impact.
- Setup: Hold an alignment stick alongside the shaft, extending past the grip, so it lightly touches the ribs. Make hip-height swings, then hit balls, then repeat without the stick. Suggested 10 to 15 minutes.
- Balls: unknown. Facility: chipping area.
- Source: [Practical Golf, Chipping Drills](https://practical-golf.com/chipping-drills) [Read]

**D-AG3 Line in the Sand**
- Purpose: Train where the club enters the sand, named as the main source of inconsistency in greenside bunkers.
- Setup: Scrape a line in the sand and place a ball at the far end, toward the inside of the lead foot. Page partly read; article is a paid partnership.
- Balls: unknown. Facility: practice bunker.
- Source: [Golf Monthly, Ged Walters (Top 50 Coach)](https://golfmonthly.com/tips/banish-bad-bunker-shots-with-these-3-expert-drills) [Read, truncated]

**D-AG4 Bunker Divot Control**
- Purpose: Consistent contact between club, sand and ball on 10 to 20 yard shots.
- Setup: Draw a line and strike the sand about an inch past it where the ball would sit, then add an alignment stick and a ball to limit how much sand is taken.
- Balls: unknown ("a few times"). Facility: practice bunker.
- Source: [GolfMagic, Andy Roberts (editor, no coaching credential given)](https://www.golfmagic.com/node/98793) [Read]

## Putting

Tees are simple to use on a putting green, and most Games need no equipment beyond them.

### Games

**G-PT1 Seven Stations** (published as "21 Around the World")
- Rules: Seven tees in a circle, 3 to 5 feet out by skill level; one putt per station.
- Scoring: 3 per make, maximum 21. Ratings: 18 to 21 excellent, 15 to 17 good, 12 to 14 average, under 12 needs practice.
- Balls: 7 putts. Time: unknown. Facility: putting green.
- Scale: station distance (source); consequences such as stay until 18+, restart after two misses, finish only by holing the last (source).
- By eye: Yes.
- Source: [GOLF.com, Two putting games](https://golf.com/instruction/putting/two-putting-games-test-performance-under-pressure/) [Read]

**G-PT2 Spiral Stations** (published as "Seashell Putting Challenge")
- Rules: Tees in a spiral at 3, 4, 5 up to 12 feet; one putt each, moving on regardless. Three misses in a row restart.
- Scoring: None published beyond holing all stations; makes or attempts could be used (inferred).
- Balls: 10 putts per pass. Time: unknown. Facility: putting green.
- Scale: green slope, about 1% basic and 1.5 to 2% advanced (source); distances (inferred).
- By eye: Yes.
- Source: [GOLF.com, Two putting games](https://golf.com/instruction/putting/two-putting-games-test-performance-under-pressure/) [Read]

**G-PT3 Long-Putt Proximity** (published as "Closest to the Hole")
- Rules: Ten balls about 30 feet from the hole.
- Scoring: Low wins. 0 holed, 1 within 1 foot, 2 within 2, 3 within 3, 5 beyond 3.
- Balls: 10. Time: unknown. Facility: putting green.
- Scale: distance, band sizes (inferred).
- By eye: Markers (a putter length helps).
- Source: [GolfLink, Mike Pile, 11 Putting Games](https://www.golflink.com/instruction/best-putting-games) [Read]

**G-PT4 Mixed Distance Sequence** (published as "Mix-Up")
- Rules: Tees at about 6, 12 and 20 feet on different lines. Six balls in a short, long, medium order, then the sequence again.
- Scoring: +1 per putt finishing more than 12 inches from the hole; lowest wins.
- Balls: 6. Time: unknown. Facility: putting green.
- Scale: distances, tolerance (inferred).
- By eye: Yes.
- Source: [GolfLink, Mike Pile](https://www.golflink.com/instruction/best-putting-games) [Read]

**G-PT5 Clock Streak** (published as "The Clock Face"; associated with Phil Mickelson by Practical Golf, see below)
- Rules: 12 balls about 3 feet from the hole in a clock pattern, varying distance slightly. Keep going until 12 in a row.
- Scoring: Attempts taken; or count of 12 made if too hard (source).
- Balls: 12 per pass. Time: unknown. Facility: putting green.
- Scale: consecutive target (source); distance, ball count (inferred).
- By eye: Yes.
- Source: [GolfLink, Mike Pile](https://www.golflink.com/instruction/best-putting-games) [Read]; [Practical Golf, Putting Drills Guide](https://practical-golf.com/putting-drills-guide) [Read]

**G-PT6 Pars or Better**
- Rules: Drop balls 10 to 12 feet out one at a time; birdie try, then putt out. Play 10 times or until all pars or better.
- Scoring: Implied stroke count; a harder version makes any birdie putt that stops short an automatic bogey (source).
- Balls: 10 (one at a time). Time: unknown. Facility: putting green.
- Scale: distance, reach-the-hole rule (source for the latter).
- By eye: Yes.
- Source: [GolfLink, Mike Pile](https://www.golflink.com/instruction/best-putting-games) [Read]

**G-PT7 Past but Not Far** (published as "Lag-It")
- Rules: Lay a club about 18 to 24 inches past the hole; putt from chosen distances.
- Scoring: 0 for short, or for hitting or passing the club; 1 for passing the hole without touching the club; 2 for holing. Total is the benchmark.
- Balls: unknown. Time: unknown. Facility: putting green.
- Scale: club closer to the hole makes it harder (source); distance (inferred).
- By eye: Yes.
- Source: [GolfTEC, Nick Miller](https://scramble.golftec.com/blog/2014/11/lag-putting-game/) [Read]

**G-PT8 Reverse Ladder**
- Rules: Six balls, marker about 30 feet away. First putt must finish within two feet of it; each next putt no more than two feet short of the previous and not past it. A failed putt restarts.
- Scoring: Published as complete or not; attempts could be counted (inferred).
- Balls: 6 per attempt. Time: unknown. Facility: putting green.
- Scale: more balls, or a zone of one grip length (source).
- By eye: Markers (the putter is the gauge).
- Source: [Practical Golf, Putting Drills Guide](https://practical-golf.com/putting-drills-guide) [Read]

**G-PT9 Prediction Putts** (inspired by Sergio Garcia, see below)
- Rules: From about 10 feet, three balls from different putts. Close eyes before the backswing and call the result.
- Scoring: 3 for correctly calling a make; 2 for correct direction and length; 1 for one out of two; 0 otherwise.
- Balls: 3. Time: unknown. Facility: putting green.
- Scale: distance, ball count (inferred).
- By eye: Yes, after opening the eyes.
- Source: [Practical Golf, Putting Drills Guide](https://practical-golf.com/putting-drills-guide) [Read]

**G-PT10 Golf Australia Putting Test** (named research instrument, see below)
- Rules: Six single putts on each of six concentric circles with radii 0.9, 1.5, 3.0, 4.6, 6.1 and 7.6 m.
- Scoring: More points for holing from longer range; maximum 27 per round. The point values per circle are not in the abstract.
- Balls: 36 putts. Time: unknown. Facility: putting green.
- Scale: distances (inferred).
- By eye: Yes, since it scores makes.
- Source: [Robertson, Gupta, Kremer & Burnett 2015, European Journal of Sport Science](https://vuir.vu.edu.au/26268) [Read]. Reported valid and reliable for high-level players.

**G-PT11 Putt Proximity Table** (Drive, Chip and Putt; named program, see below)
- Rules: One putt each from 6, 15 and 30 feet.
- Scoring: Per putt: 1 for more than 5 feet, 2 for 4 to 5, 5 for 3 to 4, 10 for 2 to 3, 15 for 1 to 2, 20 for 1 foot or nearer, 25 for holed.
- Balls: 3. Time: unknown. Facility: putting green.
- Scale: distances, attempts (inferred).
- By eye: Markers.
- Source: [Drive, Chip and Putt, Rules and Regulations](https://www.drivechipandputt.com/rules-and-regulations) [Read]

**G-PT12 Short and Long Circles** (published as "Short Putt" and "Long Putt")
- Rules: Five putts from 2 m, and five from 8 m, at a hole with circles of 0.5 and 1.5 m radius.
- Scoring: 1 for striking, +1 inside 1.5 m, +1 inside 0.5 m, 4 total if holed; maximum 20 each.
- Balls: 5 each. Time: unknown. Facility: putting green.
- Scale: distance, circle radius (inferred).
- By eye: Markers.
- Source: [Special Olympics PA, Level I Rules](https://specialolympicspa.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/Level-1-Individual-Skills-Compeition-Rules.pdf) [Read]

### Drills

**D-PT1 Gate** (described as made famous by Tiger Woods)
- Purpose: Return the putter to the same position at impact.
- Setup: Tees either side of the putter head with just enough room. An advanced version adds a second gate one putter length away.
- Balls: unknown. Facility: putting green.
- Source: [Practical Golf, Putting Drills Guide](https://practical-golf.com/putting-drills-guide) [Read]

**D-PT2 Rubber Band Strike**
- Purpose: Strike putts from the centre of the face.
- Setup: Wrap rubber bands around the putter head with a gap for the sweet spot; putt from about two putter lengths on a straight, level line.
- Balls: unknown. Facility: putting green.
- Source: [Practical Golf, Putting Drills Guide](https://practical-golf.com/putting-drills-guide) [Read]

**D-PT3 Halfway Gate** (published as "Croquet Golf")
- Purpose: Start line through a small window.
- Setup: A fairly straight 8 to 12 foot putt, two markers halfway just wider than a ball; roll through and in.
- Balls: unknown. Facility: putting green.
- Source: [GolfLink, Mike Pile](https://www.golflink.com/instruction/best-putting-games) [Read]

**D-PT4 Speed Bracket** (published as "Thread the Needle")
- Purpose: Speed control.
- Setup: From about 10 feet, the first putt must pass the hole, the second finish short, the third between them. Succeed and move farther away; fail and restart.
- Balls: 3 per sequence. Facility: putting green.
- Source: [Practical Golf, 5 Golf Games](https://practical-golf.com/golf-drills) [Read]

**D-PT5 Distance Ladder** (published as "Ladder Challenge")
- Purpose: Feel and touch.
- Setup: Balls at 5, 10, 15 and 20 feet; stop each in a one-foot zone past the hole.
- Balls: 4. Facility: putting green.
- Source: [PGA.com, Brendon Elliott](https://www.pga.com/story/nine-ways-to-play-better-golf-tee-to-green) [Excerpt]

## Practice science relevant to Game design

### Variable and random practice

- A [systematic review of 52 randomised trials in golf](https://frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fspor.2024.1324615/pdf) (Barzyk & Gruber 2024) [Read] found increasing contextual interference, errorless learning and an external focus superior within their categories. It warns that most studies used simple putting tasks in novices and that more than half lacked statistical power, so transfer to advanced players is unproven.
- [Mousavi et al. 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11235769/) [Read] gave 40 novices 360 putts over three days. Random-distance practice (1.22, 2.44 and 3.66 m, with no distance twice in a row) beat blocked practice on retention. Differential learning beat both on a 4 m transfer test.
- [Porter & Magill 2010](https://peeref.com/works/12591947) [Excerpt] found that novices moving from blocked to serial to random putting did better on retention and transfer than either pure schedule. Another study did not find this effect ([RBEFE](https://doaj.org/article/ef390450dc564cf6bc151f935afe8efd) [Excerpt]), so the evidence is mixed.
- For skilled golfers, the evidence is weak. A [conference abstract](https://ojs.ub.uni-konstanz.de/cpa/article/view/5306/4878) [Excerpt] suggests random practice may suit skilled golfers more, but I found no full peer-reviewed paper behind it. I found no study comparing random and blocked practice for pitching or chipping.
- Practitioners argue the same direction without data. GOLF Top 100 Teacher Eric Alpenfels and Dr. Bob Christina recommend unpredictable club choice and "transfer practice" that mirrors play ([GOLF.com](https://golf.com/instruction/10-proven-ways-make-range-practice-stick/) [Read]). The article gives no sample or results for the Pinehurst study it cites.

**Design implications (inferred):** Changing club or distance every shot, as G-AP1, G-OT4 and G-PT4 do, matches the direction of this evidence. Do not claim it is proven for a skilled golfer.

### Pressure and consequences

- [Cabral et al. 2024](https://katalog.lib.cas.cz/KNAV/EdsRecord/cmedm,38376935) [Excerpt] had 84 participants practise 300 putts over two days with or without mild pressure. The pressure-trained group held steady across later posttests, while controls worsened under pressure. This is a conceptual replication in early learners.
- [Low et al. 2021](https://shura.shu.ac.uk/25578) [Excerpt], a meta-analysis of 14 studies in sport and law enforcement, reported a medium benefit of pressure training (g = 0.67) and found no clear moderators.
- [Oudejans and Pijpers](https://research.vu.nl/en/publications/training-with-anxiety-has-a-positive-effect-on-expert-perceptual--2/) [Excerpt] found expert basketball players who practised with induced anxiety no longer deteriorated in an anxiety posttest.
- Coaches use one-chance rules, consequences and written scores. Jack Woods allows one attempt per location and one play per day ([G-AG1](https://golfdigestme.com/?p=28221) [Read]). GOLF.com lists restart conditions and stakes ([G-PT1](https://golf.com/instruction/putting/two-putting-games-test-performance-under-pressure/) [Read]). I found no study measuring whether scored practice Games transfer to Rounds.

**Design implications (inferred):** One-attempt rules, a score saved over time and a restart-on-miss variant are all published ways to add consequence. A logged score may itself add mild pressure, but no source tests this.

### Setting difficulty

- [Hodges & Lohse 2021](https://msl.kin.educ.ubc.ca/sites/default/files/publication-files/PrePrint_ChallengeFrameworkforCOACHING_dEC2021_JSS.pdf) [Read], extending Guadagnoli and Lee's challenge-point framework, describes an optimal challenge "zone" where performance has started to drop but the learner is not overwhelmed. They state there is "no simple percentage" of success that maximises learning or motivation, and note near misses help keep players engaged and that progressive increases in difficulty matter for experienced athletes too.
- [Guadagnoli & Bertram 2014](https://lida.sport-iat.de/ta/Record/4035459?lng=en) [Read, abstract record] apply the framework to golf practice under stress, focusing on contextual interference.
- GolfWRX's Eighteen and GolfTEC's Lag-It change difficulty through target size and tolerance (G-AP1, G-PT7); GOLF.com's Seashell changes it through green slope (G-PT2).

**Design implications (inferred):** Any target success rate for Proficiency scaling is a product decision, since the research does not supply a number. Parameters that change a target's size or tolerance appear most often in sources.

### Scoring reliability and by-eye accuracy

- Only a few golf skill tests have documented reliability or validity, according to a [2015 paper citing a systematic review](https://jssm.org/jssm-14-147.xml-Fulltext) [Read]. Those were built and tested on adult males. The validated tests here use 9 shots (Nine-Ball, reported as fair to good test-retest reliability in [Robertson's thesis](https://ro.ecu.edu.au/theses/2332) [Excerpt]), 27 shots (Approach-Iron) or 36 putts (Golf Australia), all with elite or high-level players. I found no reliability data for any of the informal Games.
- By-eye judgement of result has been studied only for putting. A [study of nine experts and nine novices](https://read.qxmd.com/read/29357738/estimating-putting-outcomes-in-golf-experts-have-a-better-sense-of-distance) [Excerpt] found experts more accurate at estimating where a putt stopped. I found no peer-reviewed study on estimating full-shot carry by eye.

**Design implications (inferred):** Prefer in-or-out scoring against a visible marker over distance estimates, especially on the range.

## Gaps

- **Approach Games.** Only G-AP1 fits as published, and only through a search excerpt. No established, self-scored approach Game with a proximity scale turned up.
- **Time per Game.** Not stated by any source for any Game. The only pace figure is a Drive, Chip and Putt finals bonus for shots within 40 seconds. Time will have to be estimated or measured.
- **Blocked sources.** Golf Digest, PGA.com, GolfWRX and Titleist refused direct fetching, so every [Excerpt] item needs its rules checked against the original page.
- **Missing details.** Scoring for the Nine-Ball Skills Test, the circle values in the Golf Australia Putting Test and the target distances in the Approach-Iron Skill Test were not available from the records I could read.
- **Bunker Games.** Only G-AG9 is a complete bunker-only scored Game, and it is set for beginners. It also needs a Facility the glossary does not have.
- **Skilled-golfer evidence.** Almost all practice-schedule research uses novices on simple putting.
- **Transfer.** I found no study showing practice Game scores predict Strokes Gained or Round scores. The closest is the prediction of tournament approach accuracy in G-AP5 and G-AP6.
- **Excluded for needing a partner or a source I could not verify.** Elliott's ladder, Horse, Pinseeker, Bocce and Flagstick Challenge need two or more golfers. The Bryn Parry bunker line scoring came from a press page that no longer loads, and Golf Monthly's Traffic Light Drill was truncated. A Towel Carry chipping drill appeared in PGA.com search results, but I could not tie it to a specific article, so it is left out.
